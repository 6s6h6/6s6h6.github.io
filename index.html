Facts worth knowing before your next round, and a short menu of drinks worth making well.

On the Bar

A few things worth knowing next time you're behind it, or in front of it.

  • The first cocktail, in print In 1806, a New York paper defined the word as spirits, sugar, water, and bitters — the template every classic still follows.
  • Shake or stir? Drinks with juice, egg, or cream get shaken for aeration. Spirit-only drinks get stirred, to stay silky and clear.
  • Dilution is the point A well-mixed drink is roughly a fifth water by the time it's poured. That's what opens up the aroma.
  • The jigger The hourglass-shaped double measure takes its name from an old British unit of about a glass and a half.
  • Bitters started as medicine 19th-century apothecaries sold bitters as a tonic, long before anyone realized a few dashes could fix a flat drink.
  • Vermouth is wine, not spirit It's a fortified, aromatized wine. Treat an opened bottle like one — keep it in the fridge, use it within a month or so.
  • Why bartenders twist a peel Expressing citrus oil over a glass changes the aroma of a drink more than the garnish changes how it looks.
  • Speakeasy Prohibition-era patrons were told to speak easy, quietly, about the address. The name outlived the law.

The Menu

Eight drinks worth learning properly. Master these and most of the rest of the bar is a variation on one of them.

Stirred & spirited

Old Fashioned

Rocks glass

Whiskey, sugar, bitters — the founding formula, barely changed since 1806.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz bourbon or rye
  • 1 sugar cube (or 1/4 oz simple syrup)
  • 2–3 dashes Angostura bitters
  • Orange peel, for garnish

Method

  1. Muddle the sugar cube with bitters and a splash of water in the glass.
  2. Add whiskey and one large ice cube.
  3. Stir until well chilled, about 20 seconds.
  4. Express the orange peel over the top and drop it in.

Negroni

Rocks glass

Equal parts gin, Campari, and sweet vermouth. Bitter, bright, unmistakable.

Ingredients

  • 1 oz gin
  • 1 oz Campari
  • 1 oz sweet vermouth
  • Orange peel, for garnish

Method

  1. Combine all ingredients in a mixing glass with ice.
  2. Stir until well chilled, 20–30 seconds.
  3. Strain over fresh ice into the glass.
  4. Garnish with orange peel.

Manhattan

Coupe

Rye and sweet vermouth, stirred cold and poured up. A steadying drink.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz rye whiskey
  • 1 oz sweet vermouth
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters
  • Brandied cherry, for garnish

Method

  1. Combine ingredients in a mixing glass with ice.
  2. Stir until well chilled.
  3. Strain into a chilled coupe.
  4. Garnish with a cherry.

Shaken & citrus-forward

Daiquiri

Coupe

Rum, lime, sugar. Three ingredients, no room to hide a bad one.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz white rum
  • 1 oz fresh lime juice
  • 3/4 oz simple syrup

Method

  1. Shake all ingredients hard with ice.
  2. Double strain into a chilled coupe.

Whiskey Sour

Rocks glass

Bourbon, lemon, sugar, and — if you're doing it properly — an egg white.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz bourbon
  • 3/4 oz fresh lemon juice
  • 3/4 oz simple syrup
  • 1 egg white (optional)
  • Angostura bitters, for garnish

Method

  1. If using egg white, shake all ingredients hard without ice first.
  2. Add ice and shake again until well chilled.
  3. Strain into the glass over fresh ice.
  4. Finish with a few drops of bitters on the foam.

Margarita

Rocks glass

Tequila, lime, orange liqueur. Salt the rim or don't — it's not the point.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz blanco tequila
  • 1 oz fresh lime juice
  • 3/4 oz orange liqueur
  • Salt, for the rim (optional)

Method

  1. Rim the glass with salt, if using.
  2. Shake all ingredients hard with ice.
  3. Strain into the glass over fresh ice.

Tall & refreshing

Mojito

Highball

Rum, lime, mint, soda. Built to be drunk slowly in the heat.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz white rum
  • 3/4 oz fresh lime juice
  • 2 tsp sugar (or 1/2 oz simple syrup)
  • 8–10 mint leaves
  • Soda water, to top

Method

  1. Gently muddle mint with sugar and lime juice in the glass.
  2. Fill with crushed ice, add rum, and stir.
  3. Top with soda water and stir once more.
  4. Garnish with a mint sprig.

After dinner

Espresso Martini

Coupe

Vodka, espresso, coffee liqueur, shaken until it forms its own foam.

Ingredients

  • 2 oz vodka
  • 1 oz fresh espresso, cooled
  • 1/2 oz coffee liqueur
  • 3 coffee beans, for garnish

Method

  1. Shake all ingredients vigorously with plenty of ice.
  2. Double strain into a chilled coupe.
  3. Garnish with three coffee beans.
